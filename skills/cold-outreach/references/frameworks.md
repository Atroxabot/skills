# Outreach frameworks

These are skeletons for first emails, follow-ups and DMs. They draw on common practitioner methods (credit to 30 Minutes to President's Club, Josh Braun, Lavender and Becc Holland's "premise" idea), written up in our own words and checked against the 2025–2026 data in [rules.md](rules.md).

Every bracket is the user's real material or a `[placeholder]`. The example emails are **illustrative**: the companies, people and numbers are invented. Never send them as written, and never present their details as real.

What separates good from bad, in every framework:
- **The first line is about them,** not you.
- **One idea, one ask.**
- **Concrete nouns and numbers** from the user's real material.
- **It reads aloud like a person talking.**
- **Nothing is invented.**

## A. Observation → Problem → Proof → Ask (default)
```
Subject: [2–5 words about their situation]

[Observation: one specific, verifiable thing about them, and why it caught your eye]
[Problem: what that observation usually causes, in their words]
[Proof: one line, e.g. a similar company + a concrete result, or how you fix it]
[Ask: an interest question]

[Name]
[Role, Company]
[Opt-out line] [Address if US/Canada]
```
**Example** (59 words in the message, not counting the greeting, sign-off and footer):
```
Subject: 4 open SDR roles

Hi Dana,

Saw Northwind is hiring four SDRs in Austin at once. When teams ramp that many reps together, the first month usually goes to building lead lists by hand instead of calling.

We set up territory-ready lists before day one. Brightpath's last cohort was booking meetings in week two instead of week six.

Worth sending over how they structured it?

Sam Ortiz
Founder, ListLift
If this isn't relevant, just reply "no" and I won't follow up.
```

## B. Three sentences
For senior readers, or as a follow-up.
```
[Why you, why now: one sentence.]
[What we do about it, with one proof point: one sentence.]
[A one-line ask.]
```

## C. PAS (Problem → Agitate → Solve)
Keep "agitate" to one factual line: a cost, a risk or a number. Theatrical pain reads as manipulation.
```
[Problem they likely have, specific to their situation]
[One factual line on what it costs]
[How you fix it + one proof point]
[Ask]
```

## D. Trigger-event email
Triggers go stale. Use ones from roughly the last 30–90 days, and say exactly what you saw.
```
Saw [trigger: funding / new role / job post / launch / earnings remark] on [date or "last week"].
Teams at that stage usually hit [problem].
[Proof line.]
[Ask.]
```

## E. Right-person ask
```
[One line on the problem you solve, and for whom.]
Are you the right person for [specific area], or is that someone else on your team?
```
Contact one or two people per company, not the whole org chart. Never fake a referral ("[Name] told me to reach out") unless it's true.

## F. Follow-up sequence
3–5 touches over about 2–3 weeks. Stop at the first reply of any kind.

| Step | Day | What it adds | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 1 | First email (A–E) | 50–100 words |
| 2 | Day 3–4 | Reply-style bump in the same thread with a **new angle**: a different consequence of the problem, or a second proof point | 1–3 sentences |
| 3 | Day 8–10 | **A resource:** "Put together [thing] on [problem]. Want it?" (offer it; don't attach or link) | 2–3 sentences |
| 4 | Day 14–16 | **A short question** that's easy to answer, or the right-person ask | 1–2 sentences |
| 5 (optional) | Day 21+ | **A polite last touch:** "I'll stop here. If [problem] becomes a priority, I'm at [email]." | 1–2 sentences |

Never write "just checking in," "bumping this to the top of your inbox," "circling back," "did my last email get buried?" or guilt-trip breakups.

**Example step 2:**
```
Dana, one more angle: the reps who start with ready lists also ramp their call volume faster, because they're not context-switching into research. Happy to share the numbers if useful.
```

## G. LinkedIn connection note (200 characters or fewer)
```
[Specific shared context or their specific work], [one line on why connecting makes sense]. [Name]
```
**Example** (about 150 characters):
```
Hi Priya, your talk at OpsCon on cutting handoffs between CS and support stuck with me. I'm working on the same problem at a smaller scale. Sam
```
No pitch, no link. Many people send no note at all to warm contacts.

## H. First DM after connecting / X DM
```
Thanks for connecting, [Name]. [Reference to why you connected.] [A light question they can answer in a line.]
```
No pitch until they reply. 2–4 short lines. On X, reply substantively to their post first, then DM about that exchange.

## I. InMail
A short cold email: a subject line (under about 200 characters, ideally a few words), 3–5 sentences, one ask, under about 400 characters total. The reason for reaching out goes in the first line.
