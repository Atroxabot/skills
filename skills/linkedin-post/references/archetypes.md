# LinkedIn post archetypes

Twelve structures that keep working on LinkedIn. Each has:
- when to use it
- a skeleton
- what makes it work or fail

Several also have a full example. All example posts are **illustrative**: the people, companies and numbers are invented to show the structure. Never reuse their details as if they were the user's.

LinkedIn's Director of Creator Products described the split this way (2026):
- **Travel to new audiences:** topical, substantive, actionable posts. That covers archetypes 2, 3, 8, 10 and 12.
- **Deepen the existing network:** personal posts. That covers archetypes 1, 5, 6 and 7.

## 1. Story → lesson

**Use when** something specific happened that changed how the author works or thinks. This is Lara Acosta's SLAY: Story, Lesson, Actionable, You.

**Skeleton**
```
[Hook: the moment or decision, with stakes]
[Rehook: context that makes the reader need the rest]

[Story: who, what, when, where. Concrete details. Build to the turn.]
[The turn: "But..." / what happened next / what they realized]

[Lesson in one or two lines]

[2-4 actionable takeaways, numbered if they're steps]

[Close: "Remember: ..." and/or a specific question to the reader]
```

**Works when** the story has a real turn and the lesson follows from it.
**Fails when** the story is a thin pretext for a generic moral ("...and that's when I realized: kindness is the ultimate KPI").

**Example**
```
I almost fired our best engineer in her first month.

She'd shipped nothing in three weeks. Every standup: "still digging."

I'd already drafted the conversation in my head.

Then she sent a 4-page doc. She'd traced our checkout bug, the one that had burned two senior devs and three sprints, to a race condition in a library we didn't even know we depended on.

The fix was 11 lines.

What I got wrong: I was measuring output. She was building understanding first, and that's slower to see.

What I do differently now:

1. In the first 30 days, I ask "what have you learned?" before "what have you shipped?"
2. I ask new hires to write up one system they've traced end to end.
3. When someone's quiet, I ask what they're stuck on before I assume they're stuck.

She's now our staff engineer.

What's a signal you misread in a new hire, in either direction?
```
Why it works:
- The hook is under 60 characters, high-stakes and specific.
- Real details: three weeks, a 4-page doc, two devs, three sprints, 11 lines.
- The lesson comes out of the story.
- The takeaways are concrete behaviours.
- The closing question is answerable from experience.

## 2. Contrarian take

**Use when** the author disagrees with common advice and has evidence or experience behind it. Contrarian posts travel well and draw comments, but only when they are defensible. Disagreement for its own sake reads as bait.

**Skeleton**
```
[The common belief, stated plainly, then "It's wrong." or a blunt counter-claim]
[Why it matters / who it hurts]

[How the common advice fails, 2-3 specific lines from experience or data]

[What to do instead]
[Optional: short list of how]

[Acknowledge where the common advice IS right, which builds credibility. Use the author's own concession if they gave one; otherwise suggest one and flag it in the notes]

[Question inviting experienced disagreement]
```

**Works when** it's specific about *when* the common advice fails.
**Fails when** it attacks a straw man, or the "instead" is just the same advice in new words.

**Example**
```
"Always be closing" is terrible advice for B2B founders.

It made our first 20 sales calls feel like ambushes. Prospects went quiet after the demo. We blamed pricing.

Then we changed one thing: we stopped pitching in the first call.

We asked about their current process, what it cost them, and who else cared. We took notes. We didn't share a slide.

Close rate on the next 30 calls went from 1 in 10 to 4 in 10.

Closing still matters. But in a first call, the buyer is deciding whether you understand the problem. Pitching early answers a question they haven't asked yet.

Founders who sell: what's the first question you ask on a discovery call?
```

## 3. How-to / listicle

**Use when** the author has a repeatable process. These earn saves, which count for far more than likes.

**Skeleton**
```
[Hook: outcome + timeframe or constraint]
[One line of credibility/context]

[3-10 numbered steps, one line each, concrete actions with specifics]

[The step that matters most, and why]

[Optional P.S. or question]
```

**Works when** each step is a concrete action ("Send the agenda 24 hours before, with one question you need answered").
**Fails when** the steps are abstractions ("Be intentional," "Communicate clearly"), all the same length and interchangeable.

## 4. "I did X, here's what I learned"

**Use when** the author has a result from sustained effort. This is Justin Welsh's most-used shape.

**Skeleton**
```
[Line 1: the result or number, short]
[Line 2: context showing it took time / wasn't easy]
[Line 3: "Here are [N] lessons:" or similar]

[Lessons: each one a specific claim + 1-2 lines of why/how]

[TL;DR or the one lesson to remember]
[Call to conversation: a question, or "happy to answer questions below"]
```

**Example**
```
I've hired 31 people in 4 years.

8 of them didn't work out. Every one of those 8 taught me something the 23 didn't.

5 lessons:

1. References lie by omission. Ask "what would you have them work on next?" not "were they good?"

2. A work trial beats a fourth interview. Two paid days told us more than six hours of talking.

3. Speed signals respect. The candidates we lost, we lost in the gap between rounds two and three.

4. Hire for the next 18 months, not the next 5 years. The "future leader" profile kept missing what we needed now.

5. Write down why you said yes. Rereading it at 90 days is the best calibration tool I have.

If I had to keep one: the work trial.

What's the hiring practice you'd never give up?
```

## 5. Before/after transformation

**Use when** there is a clear, honest change in the author's situation, team or approach.

**Skeleton**
```
[Compressed before/after hook: "[Then]: X. [Now]: Y."]

[The turning point: what triggered the change]

[3 specific things that changed, with real numbers if possible]

[What didn't work along the way / what it cost]

[Takeaway for someone at the "before" stage]
```

**Works when** it shows the cost and the messy middle.
**Fails when** it's a hero arc with no friction. That reads as a humblebrag or a sales pitch.

## 6. Mistake / failure

**Use when** something went wrong and the author learned a specific thing from it. Real failure posts earn more trust than success posts, but only if the details are real and the author owns the mistake.

**Skeleton**
```
[Hook: the specific failure + its cost]

[What I believed at the time and why it seemed reasonable]
[What happened]
[Root cause: honest, not blaming others]

[What I do now, concretely]

[Invite readers' versions: "What's a mistake that changed how you work?"]
```

**Fails when** the "failure" is really a brag ("My biggest weakness is caring too much"), or it's turned into a moral about something unrelated.

## 7. Behind the scenes / process reveal

**Use when** the author can show how their team actually does something: a doc, a meeting ritual, a tool setup, a pricing decision. This works well with an image of the real artifact.

**Skeleton**
```
[Hook: "Here's exactly how we [do X]" + a surprising specific]

[The process in steps, with real tools, numbers, time spent]

[What we'd change / what we're still unsure about]

[Question: how do others do it?]
```

## 8. Framework breakdown

**Use when** the author has a mental model or method they use repeatedly. Ship 30 calls this "analytical" content. It earns saves, and it often works even better as a document/carousel post.

**Skeleton**
```
[Hook: the problem the framework solves, or the framework's result]

[Name the framework (simple: letters, steps, or a 2x2)]

[One line per element: what it means + a concrete example]

[A worked example applying it to a real situation]

[When NOT to use it]
```

**Works when** it includes a worked example and the limits of the framework.
**Fails when** it's a made-up acronym with abstract letters ("C.L.A.R.I.T.Y.") and no application.

## 9. Observation / hot take

**Use when** the author keeps noticing a pattern. These can be short (400–800 characters), and they draw comments because people want to add their own examples.

**Skeleton**
```
[The pattern, stated sharply]

[2-3 concrete examples of it]

[Why it matters / what it reveals]

[Open question]
```

**Example**
```
The best operators I know all have one habit in common: they write things down before the meeting, not after.

A one-paragraph "here's what I think we should decide" doc, sent the night before.

The founder who does this gets 20-minute meetings. The VP who doesn't schedules a follow-up to the follow-up.

Writing first forces the thinking. The meeting is just for the disagreements.

What's a small habit you've noticed in people who get a lot done?
```

## 10. Data / result

**Use when** the author has numbers from their own work, a survey or an analysis. This is very topical and travels to new audiences.

**Skeleton**
```
[Hook: the headline number or most surprising finding]

[Method in one line: what was measured, how many, when]

[3 findings, each with its number]

[The surprising one, expanded]

[What it means for the reader + question]
```

**Works when** it states the method and sample size honestly.
**Fails when** it inflates the data or hides how thin it is. Never invent or round up numbers the user didn't give.

## 11. Celebrate others / case study

**Use when** someone else did something worth learning from: a colleague, a customer, a peer. It builds goodwill and is rarely seen as self-promotion.

**Skeleton**
```
[Hook: the specific thing they did and its result]

[What they did, concretely]
[Why it worked]

[The lesson for the reader]

[Tag them with a reason to reply, e.g. "@Name, I still use your template"]
```

Tag only the people the post is about.

---

## 12. News + take

**Use when** something changed in the industry and the author has an informed view. Timeliness helps it reach new people.

**Skeleton**
```
[Hook: what changed, as a fact, + the author's one-line stance]

[What it means for [specific role/industry], 2-3 concrete implications]

[The author's prediction or recommendation]

[Question: how is this affecting your team?]
```

**Works when** the facts are accurate and sourced, and the take adds something the headline didn't.
**Fails when** it just restates the news, or claims certainty the author doesn't have.

## Idea generation

When the user has a topic but not an angle, run it through Ship 30's 4 A's to find four different posts:

| Lens | Question | Example on "async work" |
|---|---|---|
| Actionable | How do you do it? | "How we run a 40-person team with 2 meetings a week" |
| Analytical | What do the numbers or parts show? | "We tracked meeting hours for 6 months. Here's what changed." |
| Aspirational | Show it's possible | "A year ago I was in meetings 30 hours a week." |
| Anthropological | Why do people do it? | "Why managers hold meetings they know are useless" |

Then match the angle to an archetype above.
