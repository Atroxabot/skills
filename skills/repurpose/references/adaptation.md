# Adapting a piece to each platform (as of October 2026)

This file is the per-platform guide for the repurpose skill. If the linkedin-post skill is installed, its rules for LinkedIn posts are more detailed; follow them for those pieces.

## 1. Hard limits

| Platform | Text limit | Visible before the cut | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn post | 3,000 characters | ~140 mobile / ~210 desktop | No hashtags performs best |
| LinkedIn carousel (document post) | n/a | the cover slide | PDF, up to 300 pages / 100 MB; links inside don't work; 8–12 slides is the usual sweet spot |
| X | 280 weighted (Premium: 25,000) | ~280 on long posts | Weighted: emoji and CJK count 2, every URL counts 23. The first post of a thread carries the distribution. 0–1 hashtags. |
| Threads | 500 characters | n/a | One topic tag per post; a text attachment holds up to 10,000 |
| Bluesky | 300 graphemes | n/a | Posts can't be edited; links are shortened in the count; 0–2 hashtags; alt text expected |
| Instagram caption | 2,200 characters | ~125 characters | Hard cap of 5 hashtags (caption and comments together); carousels up to 20 slides; caption links aren't clickable |
| Reels / Shorts / TikTok | n/a | the first frame + first line | Reels and Shorts are recommended up to 3 min; under 60 s fits everywhere |

## 2. What changes, element by element

| Element | How it changes |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | Rewrite for each platform's cut and culture. X: the text shows before the image, so lead with the text. LinkedIn: the first 2–3 lines decide "see more." Instagram: the image or cover slide is the hook, and the caption's first line supports it. Video: frame 1, the on-screen text and the first spoken line. |
| **Length and structure** | A blog section becomes a 900–1,800-character LinkedIn post, a 1–3-sentence X post or a 5–12-post thread, a Threads post of 500 characters or fewer, a Bluesky post of 300 graphemes or fewer, an 8–12-slide carousel, or a 30–60 s script. Cut points rather than compressing all of them. |
| **Tone** | LinkedIn: first person, professional, lesson-driven. X: compressed, opinionated, witty. Threads: conversational, warmer. Bluesky: plain, dry, community-minded. TikTok: spontaneous. Reels: a little more polished. |
| **Links** | Every piece must deliver without the click. LinkedIn: in the first comment if reach matters. X: in the body with context, or in the last post of a thread. Instagram: the bio or a DM (captions don't link). Bluesky: welcome, with your take. |
| **@handles** | Handles differ by platform. Look each one up, because a copied handle points to nobody or to the wrong person. |
| **Hashtags** | LinkedIn 0. X 0–1. Threads 1 topic tag. Bluesky 0–2. Instagram 0–5, specific. Never carry one platform's block to another. |
| **Call to action** | Match what the platform rewards. LinkedIn: a specific question for comments. X: something worth sharing or quoting. Instagram: worth saving or sending. Never "comment X to get…" bait. |
| **Platform leftovers** | Strip "link in bio" (off Instagram), "retweet", "RT" and 🧵 (off X), "see more" (off LinkedIn), "swipe" (off carousels), and features one platform has and another doesn't. |
| **Format** | A list becomes a carousel. A story becomes a text post. A moment becomes a clip. A data point becomes a chart plus one line. Resize visuals for each platform, and export video without other apps' watermarks. |

## 3. Per-platform cheat sheet

**LinkedIn post**
- **Hook:** a short, specific claim or moment within ~140 characters. Claims beat questions.
- **Body:** one idea; 150–300 words; short paragraphs with varied rhythm; three or more concrete specifics; show the cost.
- **Ending:** a takeaway, plus an optional specific question.
- **Formatting:** plain text (no markdown), 0–1 emoji, no hashtags.
- **Avoid:** "It's not X, it's Y," one sentence per line ("broetry"), and engagement bait.

**LinkedIn carousel**
- **Cover:** a specific promise in 4–10 words.
- **Slides:** one idea per slide, under ~50 words, with headings that carry the argument on their own.
- **Recap:** a recap slide near the end.
- **No links** (they don't work inside the PDF) and no "comment for the PDF" bait.
- **Post text:** names the topic and the audience, and gives a reason to open it.

**X**
- **Payoff in the first line.** The feed shows text before the image.
- **Compression:** cut the draft by about a third.
- **What to write:** something people would copy-link, DM, reply to or quote. One idea per post.
- **Threads on X:** the first post must stand alone, because it carries the distribution. Number posts only if order matters, and put links in the last post.
- **Tags:** no hashtags, or one at most.

**Threads**
- **Length:** up to 500 characters for a full thought, in a warmer, conversational tone.
- **Tags:** one topic tag.
- **Replies:** invite specific replies, and plan to answer them.
- **Images:** add a relevant one when you can.

**Bluesky**
- **The post:** complete and plain, 300 graphemes or fewer.
- **Links:** fine, with your take.
- **Images:** alt text on every one.
- **Avoid:** hype and bait. The audience is allergic to marketing voice.
- **No editing,** so proofread.

**Instagram**
- **First line:** names the topic in searchable words within ~125 characters, because captions are search signals.
- **Length by format:**
  - Reels: short (one or two lines plus keywords).
  - Carousels: the caption frames the slides.
  - Photos: a short "micro-blog" when there's a story.
- **Call to action:** at most one, tied to a use ("save this for your next…").
- **Hashtags:** 0–5, specific. Broad tags (#reels, #explore) don't help.
- **Alt text:** include it.

**Short video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts)**
- **Hook:** frame 1, the on-screen text (3–8 words) and the first spoken line (6–8 words) all say the same thing within 3 seconds.
- **Pacing:** about 2.5–3 spoken words a second (150–180 a minute), and a visual change every 2–5 seconds.
- **Captions** on the talking parts, since many people watch with the sound off.
- **Ending:** a loop back to the start, or a call to action tied to the value.
- **A separate caption for each platform.** An AI-cloned voice or realistic synthetic footage needs a label, and paid content needs a disclosure early in the video.

## 4. Transcripts: spoken to written

**Spoken language needs rework.** It repeats, signposts ("so basically"), false-starts, wanders, saves the point for the end, and points at things on screen. Written posts need the point first and one idea per paragraph.

**The author's own words:**
- Rewrite freely in their voice.
- Keep distinctive phrasing and their signature lines.
- Don't add claims, numbers or stories they didn't say.

**Other speakers:**
- **Quotes:** words in quotation marks must be what was said. Remove only filler and false starts.
- **Otherwise:** paraphrase without quotation marks, and attribute: "Maya argued that…"
- **Context:** never cut a quote so it says something the speaker didn't mean.

**Quote test:**
- one or two short sentences
- makes sense without setup
- specific
- arguable

**Clip test:**
- starts on the hook, not the wind-up
- one idea
- ends on a payoff
- about 15–60 seconds
- no "as I mentioned"
- works with the sound off once captioned

**Timestamps:** give HH:MM:SS start and end times for every quote and clip. YouTube chapters need 00:00 first, at least three timestamps in ascending order, and chapters at least 10 seconds long.

**Auto-clipping tools** pick by pattern, not meaning. Check their picks against the clip test.
