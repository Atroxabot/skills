# Originality, reuse and attribution rules (as of October 2026)

Read this when the source includes other people's material (guests, clips, charts, screenshots), or for policy questions. Cite dates when you pass a rule on: these policies changed several times in 2024–2026.

## The pattern

Every major platform tightened originality rules in 2024–2026, and they target the same four things:

1. **Other people's content** reposted without a meaningful change.
2. **Visible watermarks or logos from another app.**
3. **The same asset posted again on the same platform:** Instagram's "already posted on Instagram," X's copypasta, LinkedIn's "recycled" posts.
4. **Templated, mass-produced output** with no substance of its own: YouTube's "inauthentic content," LinkedIn's "repetitive, low-substance" posts.

**None of them penalizes an author for putting their own idea on several platforms.** The penalty is almost always on recommendations to non-followers, not removal.

## Platform by platform [Official; verified 2026-10-14 unless noted]

- **Instagram** (2026-04-30):
  - Accounts that mainly post unoriginal photos, carousels or Reels "will no longer be shown in places where we recommend content."
  - **What's original:** "Original content is work that you wholly created or reflects your unique perspective."
  - **What counts as a change:** on-image text that adds context, new graphics with new information, remix.
  - **What doesn't:** "a border, watermark, subtitles, or a credit in the captions."
  - Reels with other apps' logos aren't recommended. Your own logo is fine (Mosseri).
  - **Permissions:** "you are responsible for obtaining any necessary permissions before using someone else's work."
- **Facebook** (2025-07-14 and 2026-03-13):
  - **Penalized:** reusing others' content without "permission or meaningful enhancements."
  - **Not enough:** "simply stitching together clips or adding your watermark."
  - **Also unoriginal:** borders, inserted captions and speed changes.
  - **Duplicates:** distribution of copies is reduced.
- **YouTube:**
  - **Reused content:** allowed only with "a meaningful difference" (critical review, commentary, storyline).
  - **"Inauthentic content"** (renamed 2025-07-15): templated, mass-produced material.
  - **Shorts** (2026-10-01): reach is reduced for content "re-uploaded from other creators without adding anything of your own."
  - **Watermarks:** Shorts downloaded from YouTube Studio carry a YouTube watermark. Export clean files instead.
- **TikTok** (Community Guidelines effective 2026-09-24):
  - **Posting rights:** "You should only post content you created or have the right to share."
  - **Not recommended:** "Reused or unoriginal content posted without creative edits, such as clips that show someone else's watermark or logo."
- **LinkedIn** (2026-03-12):
  - "We're reducing recycled and click-driven posts."
  - "Recycled" isn't defined. The safe reading: don't repost an old post word for word; rewrite it with a new hook, example or angle.
- **X** (copypasta policy, via secondary sources):
  - **Limited:** identical or near-identical posts.
  - **Exempt:** quotes and copies with "unique commentary."
  - **Aggregators:** X has cut payouts to accounts that re-upload others' content. Use Share Video or Quote for attribution.
- **Threads and Bluesky:** no specific cross-posting rule was found. Threads follows Instagram's guidelines ("Post only your own photos and videos").

## Meaningful change: what counts

| Counts | Doesn't count |
|---|---|
| Your own commentary, analysis, voiceover or storytelling | Borders, captions or subtitles, speed changes |
| On-image text or graphics that add new information | Adding your watermark to someone else's work |
| Remix or reaction with real commentary | A credit in the caption |
| Material you filmed, wrote or produced yourself | Narrating what's on screen, minor technical edits, template changes |

## Attribution and permission

- **A credit is not permission.** The US Copyright Office: "There are no legal rules permitting the use of a specific number of words… or percentage of a work," and "in cases of doubt, the Copyright Office recommends that permission be obtained." Fair use weighs four factors: purpose (including whether the use is transformative), the nature of the work, the amount used, and the effect on the market.
- **Podcast and interview guests:** a signed guest release commonly covers editing, distribution, social clips, and use of the guest's name and likeness. Ask the user to confirm one exists before clipping a guest.
- **Native sharing tools** keep credit attached: Instagram Collabs and Remix, X Quote, YouTube's Posts tab, TikTok Stitch and Duet.
- **Quoting real people:** journalism's bar is the right one. Quotes are accurate, fair and in context (SPJ Code of Ethics).
